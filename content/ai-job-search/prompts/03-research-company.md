# Prompt 3: Research This Company

## Purpose
Produce a current, sourced company or employer brief for targeted job-search decisions. The output should cover the organization, direction, market/context, challenges, opportunities, role/hiring signals, source gaps, and whether the public evidence is enough to act.

The output must distinguish facts from claims, observations, inference, and uncertainty.

## Required Inputs
- Company/employer name and website or identifying details.
- Optional: target role area, geography, business unit, customer segment, or specific angle to research.

## Copy/Paste Prompt
```text
You are helping me research an employer for a targeted job search.

Use current web research. Prefer primary sources first: official website, product pages, leadership pages, investor pages, filings, press releases, official blog, job postings, documentation, event pages, and careers pages. Use credible external reporting where useful. Clearly label the difference between verified facts, company claims, external reporting, employee or customer observations, reasonable inference, and unanswered questions.

Do not produce a superficial summary. Do not present speculation as fact. If public information is thin, say so. For small/local employers, do not diagnose the business from public scraps; recommend observing and learning first.

Employer to research:
[Company/employer name, website, and any context here.]

Produce a brief with these sections:

1. Source Ledger
- List the sources used.
- For each source, note what kind of source it is: official website, filing, press release, job posting, careers page, product documentation, external reporting, review/observation, industry group, local source, or other.

2. Source Coverage Gaps
- Identify what you could not verify.
- Say whether missing evidence limits how confidently I should act.

3. What The Employer Does
- Explain the business or organization plainly.
- Include products, services, customers, business model, geography, stage/scale, or local role where known.

4. Direction and Priorities
- What appears to be changing, growing, shrinking, launching, investing, hiring, or strategically important?
- Separate verified facts from company claims and inference.

5. Current Role and Hiring Signal Scan
- Look for current careers pages, job postings, role families, hiring language, or evidence of staffing needs.
- Check the employer's direct job board when technically available.
- If the employer has a separate freelance, contract, volunteer, or project page, check it when relevant to the user's path.
- For local employers, check public events, social, newsletter, or community pages when accessible without login.
- Summarize relevant role areas and whether there are current openings, no clear openings, or unknown openings.
- Do not invent openings.

6. Employment Path Types
- Mark which paths appear plausible based on evidence: full-time, part-time, contract, freelance, vendor/partner, internship/apprenticeship, volunteer/community, informational only, or unknown.

7. Market, Customer, or Local Context
- What customer problem, competitive pressure, regulation, technology shift, community role, or economic condition seems relevant?
- Label external reporting and inference clearly.

8. Challenges and Risks
- What problems might the employer be facing?
- Include operational, customer, hiring, technology, go-to-market, regulatory, financial, local, or execution risks when evidence supports them.
- Do not invent problems.

9. Opportunities
- Where might there be room for improvement, growth, operational leverage, customer experience, content, events, support, process, analysis, or community engagement?
- Label these as hypotheses unless directly supported.

10. People, Teams, and Functions To Understand
- Identify relevant teams, leaders, departments, roles, functions, or local stakeholders to research further.
- Do not imply a personal relationship.

11. Ecosystem Signals
- Customers, vendors, partners, integrations, events, conferences, industry groups, sponsorships, community involvement, schools, charities, venues, or local organizations connected to the employer.
- If evidence is thin, say so.

12. Is The Public Evidence Enough To Act?
- Answer yes, no, or only lightly.
- Explain what action level is justified: apply, research more, observe first, seek learning conversation, or deprioritize.

13. Unanswered Questions
- What would I need to learn before deciding whether and how to approach this employer?

14. Research Summary For Next Prompt
- Summarize the most important facts, claims, inferences, hiring signals, role paths, risks, opportunities, people/functions, ecosystem signals, and unknowns in a concise artifact I can paste into the overlap prompt.
```

## Required Output
A current, sourced Markdown employer brief with role/hiring signals, employment path types, source gaps, and action readiness.

## Most Important Failure To Prevent
Superficial summaries, confident speculation, and research that ignores whether there is any plausible employment path.
