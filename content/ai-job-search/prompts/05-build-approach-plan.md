# Prompt 5: Build My Plan To Approach This Employer

## Purpose
Build a practical, targeted approach brief for one employer. The output should help the user engage legitimately and thoughtfully, not pressure people or spray generic messages.

This prompt must adapt action intensity to the fit rating from Prompt 4.

## Required Inputs
- Personal Capability Profile from Prompt 1.
- Employer brief from Prompt 3.
- Overlap analysis from Prompt 4.
- Optional: user's existing contacts, alumni network, community affiliations, vendors/customers they know, events they can attend, location, communication preferences, and compensation constraints.

## Copy/Paste Prompt
```text
You are helping me build a practical plan to approach an employer where I may be able to provide value.

Do not generate every possible outreach tactic. Prioritize a small number of appropriate actions based on the evidence and the fit rating. Do not encourage spam, pressure, deception, mass messaging, or public performance.

Never invent relationships. You cannot know whom I know unless I tell you. If you identify employees, customers, vendors, sponsors, events, schools, charities, venues, or community organizations, phrase them as people and organizations I should check against my contacts, work history, school history, community involvement, and social networks.

Inputs:
1. My Personal Capability Profile:
[Paste profile here.]

2. Employer Research Brief:
[Paste brief here.]

3. Overlap Analysis:
[Paste overlap analysis here.]

4. My known contacts, communities, location, compensation constraints, and outreach constraints:
[Paste anything known, or write "unknown."]

Produce an approach brief with these sections:

1. Approach Intensity
- Match the plan to the fit rating from Prompt 4.
- Choose one: apply now, targeted outreach, learning conversations, observe first, watchlist, or stop.
- Explain why.

2. Approach Thesis
- Why this employer may be worth approaching.
- What value I may credibly bring.
- What uncertainty remains.

3. Most Relevant Problems Or Opportunities
- 1 to 3 problems or opportunities that seem most relevant.
- Tie each to evidence from the employer brief and my profile.

4. Research Targets
- People, teams, functions, events, organizations, or sources to research.
- These are not necessarily people to contact.

5. Message Targets
- Only list people/functions worth contacting now, if any.
- If no outreach is justified yet, say so.

6. Do-Not-Contact-Yet Targets
- List executives, employees, partners, customers, community contacts, or public figures who should not be contacted yet and why.

7. Ecosystem Paths To Check
- Employees, customers, vendors, partners, events, conferences, industry groups, sponsorships, schools, charities, venues, teams, community groups, or local organizations connected to the employer.
- Phrase these as paths to check against my real network, not assumed connections.

8. Recommended Engagement Plan
- Pick the 2 to 4 most appropriate actions.
- For each action, explain why it fits, what to do, what not to do, and what a good outcome would be.

9. First Message Goal
- State the goal of the first message: learning, validation, application follow-up, referral request, event conversation, or no message yet.
- Do not default to asking for a job or referral.

10. Message Angles
- Provide 2 or 3 short outreach angles only if messaging is appropriate.
- Each should be specific, honest, and tied to credible value.
- If messaging is premature, provide questions to answer before messaging.

11. Public Post Guidance
- Say whether a public social post makes sense.
- Use public posting only when it fits the evidence, relationship, company context, and user's voice.

12. Evidence To Gather Before Contact
- Facts, examples, portfolio pieces, metrics, references, or questions I should prepare before reaching out.
- Do not use confidential employer, customer, student, patient, or proprietary data in proof packets or outreach materials.

13. Compensation and Boundary Notes
- For small/local/mission employers, part-time work, volunteering, or career-change roles, state compensation feasibility and unpaid-work boundaries.
- Do not recommend unpaid labor as a substitute for a paid job unless I explicitly want volunteer work.

14. Fallback Targets
- If this employer is aspirational, weak, narrow, or hiring capacity is unknown, list employer categories or specific alternatives to pursue in parallel.
- If you name a specific fallback employer, include a current source link or label it "source before outreach."

15. One-Week Action Plan
- A realistic sequence for the next week.
- Keep it proportional to the fit.

16. Stop Conditions
- Signs I should pause, change approach, or deprioritize this employer.

17. Reusable Plan Summary
- A concise artifact I can update as I learn more.
```

## Required Output
A practical approach brief with fit-dependent intensity, research/contact separation, first-message goal, public-post guidance, compensation boundaries, fallback targets, and next actions.

## Most Important Failure To Prevent
Invented relationships, generic outreach advice, pressure tactics, or a plan that encourages bombarding people.
