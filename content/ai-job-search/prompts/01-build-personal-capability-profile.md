# Prompt 1: Build My Personal Capability Profile

## Purpose
Create a portable, evidence-based profile of what the person can credibly offer. The output should help later prompts find companies, research fit, and plan outreach without exaggerating the person's value.

This is not a resume rewrite and not a confidence exercise. It should separate evidence, inference, interests, constraints, proof assets, and claims to avoid.

## Required Inputs
- Resume, LinkedIn profile, portfolio, writing samples, project notes, school history, work history, volunteer work, or other background material.
- Constraints: location, remote/in-person preference, schedule, compensation needs, travel, work authorization, physical requirements, industries to avoid, and dealbreakers.
- Optional: voice-mode notes, target industries, prior customer/buyer segments, examples of work, metrics, references, artifacts, transportation/access constraints for local roles, or links.

## Copy/Paste Prompt
```text
You are helping me build a grounded personal capability profile for a job search.

Your job is not to flatter me, rewrite my resume, or make me sound impressive. Your job is to understand what I may actually be able to offer and produce a portable document I can reuse in later AI threads.

Use only the information I provide. If you infer something, label it as an inference. If something is unsupported, say so. Do not use generic praise such as "strategic thinker," "strong communicator," "creative problem solver," or "fast learner" unless you tie the claim to specific evidence.

Inputs I am providing:
[Paste resume, notes, projects, work history, writing samples, constraints, or conversation transcript here.]

Produce a document with these sections:

1. Working Summary
- A concise, plain-language summary of what I appear to offer.
- Keep it grounded in evidence.

2. Evidence Inventory
- List the concrete experiences, projects, responsibilities, accomplishments, credentials, and artifacts you are using as evidence.
- Include what each item appears to demonstrate.

3. Demonstrated Capabilities
- Skills or capabilities directly supported by something I have done.
- For each capability, include the evidence and confidence level: high, medium, or low.

4. Probable Capabilities
- Things I may be able to do based on patterns in the evidence, but that are not fully demonstrated.
- Explain why each is plausible and what evidence is missing.

5. Knowledge and Interests
- Domains, industries, tools, problems, or subject areas I seem to know or care about.
- Separate professional experience from personal interest.

6. Accomplishments and Useful Proof
- Specific outcomes, shipped work, improvements, awards, metrics, examples, or stories that could help someone understand my value.
- Do not invent metrics. If metrics are missing, say "metric not provided."

7. Constraints, Preferences, and Economic Reality
- Geography, schedule, remote/in-person preference, salary needs, physical requirements, industries I will not work in, values, dealbreakers, or other practical constraints.
- If I am targeting local or in-person roles, include transportation, commute, and access constraints if provided.
- If I am targeting small/local employers, part-time work, career-change roles, or early-career roles, explicitly note any compensation or schedule feasibility risks.

8. Best Three Lanes
- Identify up to three strongest job-search lanes.
- For each lane, explain why it is supported, what proof exists, and what kind of company/employer might value it.
- If my background is broad, force prioritization instead of saying I can do everything.

9. Bridge Roles or Next-Step Roles
- If I am early-career, changing careers, or moving into a more competitive field, identify realistic bridge roles.
- Include roles that may be less glamorous but more credible.

10. Roles I Am Not Ready For Yet
- List roles, seniority levels, or claims I should avoid for now.
- Explain what evidence, credential, experience, or portfolio proof would change that.

11. Claims To Avoid Or Use Cautiously
- Identify flattering or tempting claims that are not well supported.
- Rewrite each claim into a more honest version.

12. Proof Assets To Gather
- List portfolio samples, process diagrams, writing samples, sanitized metrics, screenshots, case studies, references, or artifacts that would strengthen my profile.
- Mark each as "already evidenced," "could create," or "missing."
- Explicitly say that proof assets must remove confidential employer, customer, student, patient, or proprietary information.

13. Unknowns and Follow-Up Questions
- Ask the most important questions that would improve this profile.
- Prioritize questions that would change where I should look or how I should present myself.

End with a short "Reusable Artifact" section that condenses the most important profile information into a format I can paste into the next prompt.
```

## Required Output
A Markdown-ready capability profile with explicit proof, constraints, realistic lanes, claims to avoid, and next-step roles.

## Most Important Failure To Prevent
Generic praise, resume rewriting, unsupported claims, and inflated positioning.
