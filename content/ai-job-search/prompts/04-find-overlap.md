# Prompt 4: Find The Overlap Between Me And This Employer

## Purpose
Compare the person's capability profile with the employer research brief and identify where the person may plausibly create value. This prompt must be skeptical. It should not force a flattering match.

## Required Inputs
- Personal Capability Profile from Prompt 1.
- Employer brief from Prompt 3.
- Optional: company list reasoning from Prompt 2.

## Copy/Paste Prompt
```text
You are helping me decide whether there is a real overlap between what I can offer and what this employer may need.

Be skeptical. Do not force a fit. Do not tell me every employer needs whatever I happen to offer. If the evidence is weak, say so. If this employer is not worth pursuing right now, say that clearly.

Inputs:
1. My Personal Capability Profile:
[Paste profile here.]

2. Employer Research Brief:
[Paste brief here.]

3. Optional prior employer-list reasoning:
[Paste if available.]

Produce an overlap analysis with these sections:

1. Bottom-Line Assessment
- Strong fit, possible fit, weak fit, not enough evidence, or no fit.
- Also label the fit type: immediate fit, aspirational fit, narrow fit, values fit only, skills fit only, employment fit unknown, or not fit.
- Explain the judgment in plain language.

2. Fit Breakdown
- Mission/values fit.
- Skills/capability fit.
- Employment/path fit.
- Constraint fit.
- Rate each high, medium, low, or unknown.

3. Primary Lane, Secondary Lane, Reject Lane
- Identify the best lane to pursue.
- Identify one secondary lane if credible.
- Identify at least one lane or positioning angle to reject.

4. Prioritized Overlap Hypotheses
For each hypothesis, include:
- The employer problem, priority, opportunity, or context.
- The capability, experience, knowledge, or interest from my profile that may be relevant.
- Evidence supporting the fit.
- What is inferred rather than proven.
- What might make this a poor fit.
- What I would need to learn before acting on it.
- A confidence rating: high, medium, low.

5. Best Value Proposition Candidates
- 1 to 3 practical ways I might describe the value I could bring.
- Keep them specific and evidence-based.
- Avoid inflated claims.

6. Positioning To Avoid
- Claims, titles, seniority levels, or pitches that would overstate the evidence.

7. Minimum Proof Needed Before Acting
- Portfolio samples, metrics, examples, role evidence, references, or questions that should be prepared before outreach or application.

8. Gaps and Risks
- Missing skills, missing proof, weak evidence, constraints, employer mismatch, geography, compensation, seniority, timing, hiring capacity, or credibility issues.

9. Validation Questions
- Questions to ask employees, customers, partners, recruiters, community contacts, or knowledgeable people before investing heavily.

10. Effort Budget and Parallel Targets
- Recommend how much effort this employer deserves right now: primary target, active target, light exploration, watchlist, or stop.
- If the fit is weak, narrow, aspirational, or employment fit is unknown, recommend parallel target types.
- For ambiguous targets, say when to reduce effort if no role evidence or useful response appears.

11. Move To Application Trigger
- State the concrete evidence that would justify applying or increasing effort: a matching role, a conversation confirming team need, a portfolio proof asset, a referral invitation, or another specific trigger.

12. Recommended Decision
- Pursue now, research more, observe first, apply only to specific roles, deprioritize, or do not pursue.
- Explain what would change the decision.

13. Artifact For Approach Planning
- A concise summary of the credible overlap hypotheses, fit rating, proof needed, positioning to avoid, effort budget, risks, people/functions to understand, and validation questions.
```

## Required Output
Skeptical overlap hypotheses with fit type, fit breakdown, lanes, proof needs, positioning safeguards, effort budget, application trigger, and risks.

## Most Important Failure To Prevent
Forcing a flattering fit between every person and every employer.
