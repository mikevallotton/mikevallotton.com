AI doesn’t think, but it is exceptionally good at generating tokens based on context.

Yesterday I talked about why context matters so much. Today let’s talk about what these models are actually good at, once they have that context.

Once you’ve given the model enough information, from different perspectives, it can start comparing those perspectives instead of just summarizing one of them. It can identify the major claims, who’s making them, what evidence supports them, what evidence challenges them, and where the evidence is actually weak.

It can also recognize when different sources are saying the same thing with different words or when they’re looking at the same facts but emphasizing different details. Sometimes a disagreement is about facts, and sometimes it’s about interpretation, and that’s a really hard thing for people to do quickly across dozens of articles. It’s something these models are really good at.

This isn’t about asking AI to decide what’s true. It’s about using AI to organize information in a way that’s difficult for people to do quickly on their own. That’s what LLMs are really good at.
