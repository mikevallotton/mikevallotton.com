AI makes writing, analysis, design, coding cheap. Things that used to take hours or days can now happen in minutes.

And when the cost of producing something collapses, the systems behave differently.

You see it in the noise explosion. When ideas are cheap to produce, organizations get flooded with them, and then the bottleneck becomes attention.

You see it in misinformation. When convincing content becomes cheap to generate, credibility gets harder to judge.

You see it in idea convergence. AI can raise the average quality of ideas, but it can also pull people toward the same answers.

You see it in trust systems. When content itself can’t be trusted, people rely more on signals like reputation.

You see it in institutional lag. Technology moves quickly, but the rules and systems around it take time to catch up.

And none of this means that AI is bad for organizations. It means the advantage used to come from the ability to produce, but increasingly it is coming from the ability to filter, decide, coordinate, and build trust. Because when execution becomes cheap, the systems around that execution become your advantage.
