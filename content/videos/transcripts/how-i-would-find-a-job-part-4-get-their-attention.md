Yesterday, we figured out what companies you might be able to provide value to. If you haven’t done that yet, go watch the last video. Do it. Come back today. We’re going to figure out how to get their attention.

For every company where you found a real meaningful overlap, go back to AI and research deeper. Who works there? Who are their customers? Who are their vendors, partners? Who runs the part of the business where you might be able to contribute? What are those people talking about? What events do they participate in? What conferences or industry groups do they attend? What organizations, charities, or community events do they support or sponsor? Where do people from this company show up? What else can you learn about what’s happening inside and around that company?

Gather as much useful context as you can, and then give AI everything we built this week: what it knows about you, what it knows about the company, and where you could provide value. Have it build you a brief. What value do you bring to this company? What problems do they have that you might be able to help solve? Who should you be talking to? What’s the best way to approach them? Are any of their employees, customers, vendors, or partners people you might already know? Where might you naturally find an opportunity to meet and engage with the people from this company?

I put a prompt on mikevallotton.com to help with this too. Read it. Once you have that brief, read it and start doing what it tells you to do. Maybe call them, reach out on LinkedIn or on other social media. Send emails, talk to people you know. Go to an event that they are sponsoring, or an industry group where their people are involved. Go there in person, Boomer. Make a post on your own social media saying that you’ve been learning about this company, and you think they’re doing something interesting, and you’d love the opportunity to work with them, and tag them in it.

And don’t go do all those things. Figure out which ones actually make sense based on what you’ve learned. The point’s not to send one perfect message and hope somebody responds. You’re trying to make it really obvious that you are interested in this company, that you’ve done your homework, that you understand where you could provide value, and that you would be thrilled to have the opportunity to prove it.
