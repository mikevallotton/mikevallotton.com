# Evidence and Source Audit for MikeVallotton.com

## Scope and method

This audit is designed to preserve Mike Vallotton’s point of view while identifying where external evidence can appropriately strengthen factual or quasi-factual claims. I prioritized the claim classes you listed, gave extra weight to explicit numbers and timelines, preferred primary or near-primary sources, and excluded generic SEO/consultancy summaries when better evidence existed. Where the claim was broader than the available evidence, I say so directly rather than stretching the source. Where I could not find a credible direct source, I record that honestly rather than recommending any change to the claim.

Because MikeVallotton.com was not fully text-indexable through the available tooling, I mapped claims to publicly surfaced Mike Vallotton page and video titles that appear to correspond to the same themes and transcripts, including **“Every industry is quietly splitting in two,” “Organizational Knowledge May Matter More Than Smarter AI for Enterprise Performance,” “AI Generated Code: Developer Accountability and Orchestration,” “AI Mediation: Measuring Content’s Hidden Influence,” “AI-Facilitated Learning Isn’t Lowering the Bar. It’s Raising the Stakes,”** and **“How AI Agents Shift Responsibility in Workflows.”** citeturn11search0turn11search1turn11search2turn11search3turn12search0turn12search1

The strongest evidence in this audit supports claims about **task-level change before occupational replacement, disproportionate exposure in white-collar work, heterogeneous productivity gains, coding-assistant productivity heterogeneity and security risks, automation bias and cognitive offloading, long-context limitations, and the importance of governance/identity/oversight in agent systems.** The weakest claims are the **exact numeric assertions** such as **15–20% organizational readiness, 18–36 month or five-plus-year timelines, 10x speedups, 100x cost reductions, and specific staffing ratios like “a project no longer needs five people.”** Public evidence exists around those topics, but the exact figures were not directly established by the strongest sources I could verify. citeturn18view3turn18view1turn20view1turn20view2turn22view0turn25view1turn26search9turn26search28turn26search19turn28search8turn32view1turn39search8turn39search11

## Gaps, weak spots, and where the evidence is strongest

The **most citation-ready claims** on MikeVallotton.com, based on the strongest verified evidence, are the claims that AI often changes **tasks before occupations**, hits **cognitive white-collar work** especially hard, yields **heterogeneous productivity gains**, creates **review/testing/security burdens in coding**, produces **automation-bias risks**, can make outputs **fluent but wrong**, and requires **organizational readiness plus governance** to produce durable value. Those areas line up well with high-quality economic research, peer-reviewed experiments, NIST guidance, and official AI/search documentation. citeturn18view3turn18view1turn20view0turn20view1turn20view2turn22view0turn25view1turn26search9turn26search19turn27search10turn28search8turn32view4turn32view5

The **claims that need the most caution in sourcing** are the explicit numeric or quasi-numeric ones: **15–20%** readiness, **18–36 months / five-plus years** adoption, **10x** speedups, **100x** cost reductions, and exact staffing-ratio claims like **“you no longer need five people.”** I found adjacent evidence for each theme, but not strong direct validation of those exact figures from the highest-quality sources I could verify. If those sentences remain on the site, the cleanest audit outcome is to note that the supporting evidence is **adjacent rather than direct**. citeturn39search11turn39search8turn41search10turn20view1turn20view2turn22view0

The **AI-search influence** claim is the trickiest. Official Google and Microsoft documentation clearly supports the facts that AI search products synthesize across sources, can answer inside the interface, and have evolving reporting behavior. Official documentation does **not** directly prove that AI-mediated exposure changed buying decisions without visits. What it does support is a narrower, defensible version: exposure, discovery, and referral are now separate measurement problems, and traditional site analytics alone are not a complete proxy for AI-mediated visibility. citeturn34view0turn34view2turn34view3turn35search0turn35search1turn35search9

The strongest overall pattern in the evidence is that AI’s real-world impact is **not** well captured by single-variable narratives. Capability advances matter, but realized outcomes depend heavily on workflow design, data quality, permissions, complementary investments, governance quality, evaluation discipline, and the amount of human review still required after the draft or answer appears.
